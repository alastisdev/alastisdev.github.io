2026.11.11 SHADOW BLOCKS PUZZLE DESIGN

The first move clears nothing in Shadow Blocks

Room 10 gives you three purple gems, two moves and a little space between them. You can see where the row should end up. The odd bit is spending your first move and watching all three gems stay on the board.

When the instruction gives too much away

In the first four rooms I show the instructions straight away. They teach you how to move a block and what happens when the blocks settle. By the tenth room, though, working out which gap to close is the puzzle. I already had a written clue for it. Showing that clue as soon as the room opened meant you could read the move before you’d even looked at the three gems.

I kept the board, the two-move limit and the original clue. What I changed was the moment the clue appears. Rooms 5 through 20 now open with “Clear all blocks.” If you want a nudge, press Hint. The blocks stay where you put them and the move counter stays put too.

Show one solution to Room 10

Slide the right-hand amethyst one space to the left. It now sits beside the middle one. Slide the left-hand amethyst one space to the right to complete the row.

Trying the same room again

On Fedora I tried Room 10 with the clue hidden and with it open. Both times the setup move used one turn and the match used the other. Opening the clue, pausing and coming back still left me looking at the same board. Restarting hid the clue again, so I could have another go on my own.

That’s the detail I kept in the Linux release. You get the first look at each of those sixteen puzzles. My clue is there if you ask for it.
